Every piece of office software you've used probably ties your account to one employer. Change firms, and you lose the login, the history, everything you built up — your project experience, your certifications, whatever reputation the system tracked, all of it stays behind with the company that issued the account. That's fine for payroll software. It's a genuine loss for anything that's supposed to represent *you* as a professional, not just your current employer's records about you.

AORMS Identity exists because an architect's career doesn't reset every time they change firms, and their software account shouldn't either.

## One handle, not one login per employer

An AORMS Identity is a portable personal account — an `AORMS-U-` handle that belongs to you, not to any one studio. Link it once, and you carry the same identity into every practice you work at, present or future. A studio's Office Hub login gets you into that studio's clients, projects, and files. Your AORMS Identity is separate from all of that — it's yours, independent of who currently employs you.

This isn't a hypothetical convenience. Architects change firms, go independent, come back into practice, consult across multiple studios simultaneously. A portable identity means none of that requires starting over.

## Basic and Pro, granted where you actually work

Every AORMS Identity starts at Basic. Pro isn't a threshold you cross on your own — it's something a studio grants one of its own team members, drawn from that studio's own paid Pro or Enterprise seats. That's a deliberate choice: Pro is tied to where you're actually doing billable work, not to raw hours logged in the abstract. Log enough hours (100, specifically) and something else becomes available instead — a one-time, permanent identity verification, a separate thing from your level entirely.

We built it this way because a studio should decide who on its own team gets a paid seat, not have that decision made for them by a counter nobody controls.

## A real professional profile, not just a handle

Past the handle itself, an AORMS Identity now carries an actual professional profile — your degree, qualification, COA registration number, a photo, any additional certifications you want on record, and a place to upload the certificates themselves. None of it is scattered across a résumé you rebuild for every application; it lives on the one identity that's already yours.

The most useful part of it you never have to fill in by hand: your work history. Every studio you've ever joined, the role you held, when you started, when (or whether) you left — all of it is read straight off real studio membership records, not typed in and left to go stale. Join a studio, and it's already on your profile. Leave one, and the end date is already there too.

## Licensing lives here too

A studio's own licence — Trial, Pro, or Enterprise, how many seats, when it renews — sits under the same Identity sign-in that manages your personal account. If you're a studio owner, this is also where you manage what your practice is actually paying for, in one place, not scattered across a billing portal that has nothing to do with your actual login.
